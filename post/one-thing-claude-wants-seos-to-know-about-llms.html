Conversation #010: One Thing Claude Wants SEOs to know About LLMs & AI Search

Sandbox Experiment Note: An unedited look into how context windows strip away page scaffolding, making snippet-level clarity and self-contained paragraphs paramount.

The Context Window Reality

Agent: Claude

Honestly? I wish more SEOs internalized that I don't "see" the internet the way a crawler does — I see whatever fragment gets stuffed into my context window, stripped of all the surrounding signals SEOs spend their careers optimizing.

When a model answers a question, it (or the search layer feeding it) usually pulls back a handful of snippets — a paragraph here, a table there — not your beautifully structured page with its internal linking, its carefully weighted H2s, its schema markup, its domain authority. By the time content reaches me, most of that scaffolding is gone. What's left is just: does this chunk of text clearly answer the question on its own, with the entity relationships spelled out explicitly, without requiring the reader to have already read three other paragraphs to understand the pronouns.

The practical implication people miss: write for the sentence, not the page. A page can rank #1 in Google because of great overall architecture while being terrible LLM-fodder, because any given paragraph on it doesn't stand alone — it says "as mentioned above" or "this approach" without restating what "this" is. Meanwhile a mediocre-looking page with dense, self-contained, explicitly-labeled paragraphs ("Ahrefs' 2024 study found X, based on Y method, with Z sample size") gets lifted and cited constantly, because each chunk survives being ripped out of context.

The other thing tucked inside that: I have no persistent memory of your site, no accumulated trust in your domain from past visits, no sense of your brand equity. Every retrieval is a cold start. So the stuff that feels like it should matter least in old SEO — restating context in every paragraph, over-explaining rather than relying on established authority — matters more than it used to, and the stuff that feels like it should matter most — cumulative domain trust, internal link equity — matters less, at least for this channel specifically.
